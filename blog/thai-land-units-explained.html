Thai land units explained: rai, ngan and wa

LandSeptember 14, 2026·2 min read

By UnitPulse team

Listings, title deeds and family conversations in Thailand still quote land in rai, ngan and wa. The sizes are exact metric quantities, and they nest cleanly once you have seen the ladder once.

The ladder

A wa (ว้า) is a length of 2 metres. A square wa (ตารางวา) is therefore 2 × 2 metres, which is 4 square metres. People often say “wa” when they mean square wa. On a land figure, that is the safe reading.

A ngan (งาน) is 100 square wa. That is 400 square metres. A rai (ไร่) is 4 ngan, or 400 square wa, or 1,600 square metres. Those three sentences are the whole system:

  • 1 square wa = 4 m²
  • 1 ngan = 100 square wa = 400 m²
  • 1 rai = 4 ngan = 400 square wa = 1,600 m²

How a combined figure is written

Someone says “2 rai 1 ngan 50 wa” and means 2 rai, plus 1 ngan, plus 50 square wa. Add the pieces. Do not multiply them together.

2 × 1,600 = 3,200 m². One ngan adds 400 m². Fifty square wa add 50 × 4 = 200 m². The plot is 3,800 m². The same method works in the other direction: divide square metres by 4 to get square wa, then take out groups of 400 square wa (a rai) and 100 square wa (a ngan).

Another worked figure: 2 rai 3 ngan 50 square wa is 3,200 + 1,200 + 200 = 4,600 m².

Acres and hectares

A hectare is 10,000 m², which is 10,000 / 1,600 = 6.25 rai. An international acre is about 4,047 m², or roughly 2.53 rai. A rai is much closer to a large suburban garden, or a bit under half an acre, than to a farm field in the way an English speaker might hear “acre”.

What this does not do

These are the standard metric equivalents used for conversion. A title deed, a survey pin and a fence line can disagree for reasons that a calculator cannot see. Use the converter to understand a listing or a conversation. Use a survey and the land office when the boundary itself matters.
