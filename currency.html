FAQ

Where do the rates come from?

From Frankfurter, using European Central Bank reference rates. Those rates are published on business days. They are a standard mid-market reference, not a price you can trade at.

Why is this different from my bank or a currency booth?

A reference rate has no spread. A bank, card or bureau buys and sells at different prices and may add a fee. Weekend and airport rates are often further from the reference. Use this figure to judge an offer, not as the amount a transfer will settle for.

What does “approximate, offline” mean?

The page saves the last rates it fetched. If you are offline after that, it uses the saved rates and says so. If this browser has never saved any, it uses a table of European Central Bank rates from 9 October 2026 built into the page, and it labels that table approximate, offline.

Which currencies can I convert?

The quick row is baht, euro, US dollar, pound, yen, yuan, Australian dollar and Singapore dollar. The lists also include the rest of the currencies in the European Central Bank reference set, such as Swiss franc, baht’s neighbours, and the Nordic currencies.

Do you keep what I convert?

No server stores the amounts. This browser remembers the last currencies you picked, the decimal setting, and the cached rates. Clearing site data for UnitPulse removes them.
